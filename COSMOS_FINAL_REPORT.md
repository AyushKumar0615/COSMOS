# COSMOS — Overnight Infinite Universe Evolution: final report

Date: 2026-11-12. Branch `main` on `e1a817e`; everything below is uncommitted,
unpushed and undeployed. Checkpoint detail is in `COSMOS_OVERNIGHT_PROGRESS.md`.

## 1. Implemented features

**Solar system bodies**
- 20 moons on JPL mean elements with nodal and apsidal precession: the Moon,
  Phobos, Deimos, Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto, Mimas, Enceladus, Tethys,
  Dione, Rhea, Titan, Iapetus, Miranda, Ariel, Umbriel, Titania, Oberon,
  Triton. Tidally locked, phases from real lighting, own surfaces, panels.
- Pluto–Charon binary about a barycentre outside Pluto (from JPL GM values).
- Dwarf planets Ceres, Haumea (elongated), Makemake, Eris; asteroids Vesta,
  Pallas, Hygiea; near-Earth asteroids Eros, Bennu, Apophis.
- Five comets with coma, anti-sunward ion tail and curved dust tail, active
  inside about 5 AU.
- Asteroid belt with Kirkwood gaps; Kuiper belt with plutinos at Neptune's 3:2.

**Time, dates and phenomena**
- One simulation clock: pause, four educational speeds, and real dates from
  1800 to 2050 at an hour to a year per second, with a date picker.
- Planets start at today's real longitudes and spin at JPL rotation periods.
- Eclipses from real geometry: solar, lunar (penumbral, partial, total, copper
  Moon) and moon shadows on the giant planets; banner labelled "computed for
  this date" or "simulated alignment".
- Auroras on Earth, Jupiter and Saturn (false colour for the giants).
- Coronal mass ejections from about half the solar flares.

**Spacecraft**
- Voyager 1 and 2, New Horizons and Parker Solar Probe placed from JPL Horizons
  ephemerides for the simulated date, with tracks, a findable glint, and
  distance from Sun and Earth plus radio signal time in the panel. Parker is
  interpolated with a two-body fit (closest approach 0.0462 AU vs Horizons
  0.0459). Nothing is drawn outside the downloaded span.
- Juno added at Jupiter (compressed orbit, labelled).
- New models: Voyager, New Horizons, Parker, Juno.

**Interface**
- Find panel for every body and spacecraft; body panels with JPL figures and
  source links; "Moons shown" chips in planet panels; arrow keys step peers.
- Effects menu: Orbit lines and Auroras switches.
- About page brought up to date.

**Fixes found by testing and screenshot review**
- Aurora and CME shaders squared their alpha and were invisible.
- Eclipse banners stacked; the banner overlapped the Time button on desktop.
- Universe atlas (pre-existing): the fade into a system could stop at 95%
  opacity, leaving the system black. Fixed in `AtlasExplorer.jsx`.
- Atlas moons were lit from their own planet; now sunlight, and the camera
  arrives on the sunlit side.
- Phones: the Phenomena "About …" button overlapped Exit.
- Halley's next-perihelion estimate hidden when its elements are too old.

## 3. Scientific data sources

- JPL approximate planet positions, Table 1 (elements and rates, 1800–2050).
- JPL planetary satellite mean elements and physical parameters.
- JPL Small-Body Database (full-precision elements, physical values).
- JPL Horizons heliocentric ecliptic vectors for the five spacecraft
  (retrieved 2026-11-11).
- `measured.json` (NASA, JPL, SIMBAD) for radii and rotation periods.
- NASA dwarf-planet pages for diameters JPL leaves blank.
- Meeus, *Astronomical Algorithms*, ch. 47, low-precision lunar terms.
- IAU 2015 nominal solar radius (a defined constant) for the distance map.
- NASA and Johns Hopkins APL mission pages for spacecraft facts.
Measurements are generated by `npm run orbits` / `npm run catalogue`, not typed.

## 5. Performance

- 60 fps in every measured scene on the final run (58–61 across runs) (solar, Sun and Earth close-ups,
  galaxies, nebulae, black hole, supernova, universe, system).
- First contentful paint 324 ms (local production build).
- Live JS heap 12.4 → 12.5 MB across scene cycles; GPU geometries, textures
  and programs unchanged across scene changes.
- Bundle (gzip): main 32.4 kB (was 25.8 before this mission); SceneCanvas
  22.6 kB; lazy chunks SolarExtras 5.5 kB, orbits data 4.6 kB, BodyPanel
  3.2 kB, Probes 1.7 kB, trajectories 27.9 kB (only once spacecraft show).

## 6. Remaining limitations

- Real-dates precision: JPL low-precision elements; Moon good to about 1°, so
  eclipse times within a few hours and a very shallow partial lunar eclipse
  can show as penumbral. Positions are in the J2000 frame.
- Distances and sizes are compressed; no true-scale mode yet.
- Not built: on-screen labels, measuring tool, asteroid-collision simulation,
  screen-wide bloom, Cassini and Mars rovers in the scene.
- Juno's orbit is a compressed circle; spacecraft tracks end in 2027–2029.
- Phone landscape: the atlas panel covers part of a focused moon.
- Tested only in Chrome (desktop GPU and simulated viewports); Safari and
  real phones are untested.

## 7. Features needing external data or APIs

- Longer or refreshed spacecraft tracks: rerun `npm run orbits` (JPL
  Horizons), then review the diff. Live tracking would need a runtime API and
  is deliberately not used; the site makes no data requests at runtime.
- Higher-precision ephemerides (e.g. JPL DE440) would be several MB.
- Photographic planet and moon maps (NASA public domain) would add MB.
- Mars rover positions would need mission-provided coordinates.
